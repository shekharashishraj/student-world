## Research Foundations

> Sources gathered March 2026 to ground platform design in pedagogical theory.

### Northouse Leadership Trait Approach
The trait approach (Northouse, Chapter 2) identifies **5 key traits** consistently associated with leadership effectiveness:
| Trait | Definition | Platform mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **Intelligence** | Cognitive ability, verbal fluency, perceptual ability, reasoning | "Cognitive Strength" tag option |
| **Self-Confidence** | Certainty about one's competencies, assertiveness under uncertainty | Badge: "Confident Leader" |
| **Determination** | Drive, initiative, persistence, dominance, goal-focus | Quest: "Persistence" (joins despite setbacks) |
| **Integrity** | Honesty, trustworthiness, taking responsibility | Pillar alignment: Trustworthiness |
| **Sociability** | Friendly, outgoing, diplomatic, sensitive to others' needs | "Relationship Builder" strength option |

> Note: The course syllabus also lists "Drive, Motivation, Cognitive Ability, Confidence, Integrity, Knowledge" — these are the applied OGL 200 labels for the same 5 underlying constructs plus course-specific framing.

### Tuckman's 5 Stages of Group Development
*(Bruce Tuckman 1965; 5th stage "Adjourning" added 1977 with Mary Ann Jensen)*
| Stage | What happens | Leadership challenge | Platform activity idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forming** | Uncertainty about roles, norms, expectations | Leader provides direction | Students arrive at the map, unsure where to connect |
| **Storming** | Competition, conflict, criticism of leader | Leader coaches through conflict | Professor runs "conflict style" poll |
| **Norming** | Agreement on norms, growing cohesion | Leader facilitates | "Value Ally" quest — finding shared values |
| **Performing** | Creative, high-output teamwork | Leader delegates | "Global Network" badge — 3+ connections made |
| **Adjourning** | Task completion, closure, emotional wrap-up | Leader celebrates | End-of-session spotlight, export roster |

> Research note: Leadership style should change as team matures — directive → collaborative → shared leadership. Platform can model this by unlocking more autonomous quests in later modules.

### French & Raven's 5 Bases of Power
*(French & Raven, 1959; 6th base "Informational Power" added by Raven 1965)*
| Power Base | Source of Influence | Ethics flag | Platform field option |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Legitimate** | Position/title/role | Neutral | ✅ include |
| **Reward** | Ability to offer incentives | Neutral | ✅ include |
| **Expert** | Skill, knowledge, credentials | Positive | ✅ include |
| **Referent** | Personal appeal, charisma, respect | Positive | ✅ include |
| **Coercive** | Threat of punishment | ⚠️ Flag in dashboard | ✅ include — surface for discussion |
| **Informational** | Access to unique information | Context-dependent | Optional 6th option |

> Platform design note: If a student selects "Coercive," the professor dashboard could surface this as a discussion catalyst — not as a judgment, but as a pedagogical prompt ("You have X students who lean coercive — what does Module 6 teach us about this?").

### Josephson Institute: 6 Pillars of Character
*(Used in Module 6 ethics content — maps to professor poll design)*
| Pillar | Core meaning | Quiz scenario angle |
|---|---|---|
| **Trustworthiness** | Honesty, integrity, reliability, loyalty | "Would you report a teammate's mistake?" |
| **Respect** | Non-violence, non-humiliation, non-manipulation | "How do you handle a team member who disagrees loudly?" |
| **Responsibility** | Accountability, owning consequences | "The project fails. Who's responsible?" |
| **Fairness** | Impartiality, due process, equity | "Two students did equal work. One is praised. What do you do?" |
| **Caring** | Empathy, concern for others' welfare | "A team member is struggling. What's your first move?" |
| **Citizenship** | Civic duty, community contribution | "Your team's work could harm the community. Do you proceed?" |

> These map directly to CIVI LOs: scenario-based polls where students vote → results shown by geography → class sees how cultural background shapes ethical intuition.

*(play.eigames.com — required for Modules 1–6 in levels)*

### Gamification in Leadership Education — Research Evidence
*(Systematic review: Springer 2023; MDPI 2024; Pepperdine study)*
| Finding | Implication for design |
|---|---|
| Points, badges, leaderboards → **15.1% more posts** + **25.9% longer threads** | Leaderboard + badge XP directly boosts discussion quality |
| **Short-term gamification** is more effective than long-term — motivation drops over time | Design each class session as a fresh mini-game, not a semester-long grind |
| **Narrative + mission framing** increases engagement beyond bare points | Each module should have a story hook ("This week: you are a new CEO on Day 1…") |
| Gamification systematic review of 23 articles: positive effects on **motivation, engagement, achievement** | Validated approach for leadership curriculum — not just for STEM |
| Challenges: **resource limits + evaluation difficulty** | Keep instructor tooling simple (1-click poll, 1-click quest unlock) |

## The 6 Modules — Core Content Mapping

### Module 1 · Understanding Leadership & Recognizing Your Traits
**Key Concepts**: Management vs Leadership · 7 essential leadership concepts · 6 key traits · Destructive leadership · Global leadership examples
**Assignments**: Introduction post + replies (35 pts) · Chapter quizzes · EI Games Civic Module
**Platform opportunity**:
- Student pin form: ask students to pick their **top 2 leadership traits** from the 5 (Intelligence / Self-Confidence / Determination / Integrity / Sociability — Northouse trait approach)
- Surface "trait match" when two students share the same top trait → **"Trait Twin"** badge (+25 XP)
- Discussion prompt shown on student card: *"What global leader from your country embodies these traits?"*
- AI insight angle: *"Which traits dominate in this class? What does that mean for team dynamics?"*

---

### Module 2 · Understanding Leadership Styles & Attending to Tasks and Relationships
**Key Concepts**: Authoritarian / Democratic / Laissez-faire styles · Task vs relationship orientation · Decision-making
**Civic Discussion**: "The Butter Battle Book" — structured deliberation on conflict + leadership (150 pts)
**Platform opportunity**:
- Add **Leadership Style** field to join form (3 choices: Authoritarian / Democratic / Laissez-faire) — displayed as a badge on pin cards
- Dashboard shows **class style distribution** pie/bar
- Icebreaker prompt: *"Ask [Name]: When your leadership style conflicts with someone else's — how do you bridge that?"*
- Quest: "Find a student with a different leadership style" → **Style Bridger** badge

---

### Module 3 · Developing Leadership Skills & Engaging Strengths
**Key Concepts**: Administrative skills · Interpersonal skills · Strengths-based leadership
**EI Games**: Level 1 due
**Platform opportunity**:
- Add **Top Strength** field to join form (e.g. Communication, Empathy, Strategy, Execution, Relationship, Learning)
- Show strength on pin card as a colored chip
- Connection mechanic: when students connect with someone whose strength complements theirs (e.g., Strategy + Execution), award **Complementary Strengths** badge
- Dashboard: "Strength Diversity Index" — how many unique strengths are represented

---

### Module 4 · Creating a Vision & Working with Groups
**Key Concepts**: 5 characteristics of a vision · 4 constructive climate factors · Tuckman's model (Forming→Storming→Norming→Performing) · Out-group members
**Civic Discussion**: "Leadership, Ethics and AI" (150 pts)
**EI Games**: Level 2 due
**Platform opportunity**:
- **Class Vision Wall**: professor opens a live prompt; students contribute one word to a collective vision word cloud rendered on the globe (words float above their pin location)
- **Tuckman Stage Poll**: Professor broadcasts a team scenario (e.g., "Team argues over roles, leader is challenged") → students tap which stage: Forming / Storming / Norming / Performing / Adjourning → results shown live on dashboard with globe heatmap (do students from collectivist cultures answer differently?)
- After poll: AI insight prompt — *"What does it mean that 60% of students identified this as Storming? What leadership style does Tuckman prescribe for this stage?"*
- Quest: "Identify an out-group pattern" — students flag a cultural out-group dynamic from the map interaction → earns **Group Analyst** badge
- Icebreaker: *"Ask [Name]: What's one word that defines your team's leadership vision in [country]?"*

---

### Module 5 · Embracing Diversity and Inclusion & Managing Conflict
**Key Concepts**: 6 components of inclusion experience · Barriers to D&I · Conflict management styles
**EI Games**: Level 3 due
**Platform opportunity**:
- **Inclusion Score**: when a student has connected with pins from 4+ different regions, they earn **Inclusion Champion** — shown in dashboard as "Students who reached 4+ regions: X"
- Add **Conflict Style** to profile (Collaborating / Competing / Avoiding / Accommodating / Compromising)
- Map visualization: draw lines between conflict-complementary pairs (e.g., Competing + Collaborating)
- Professor tool: "Diversity Spotlight" button — highlights the most geographically isolated student, surfaces their story for class discussion
- Quest: "Connect with someone from a different continent who has a different conflict style" → **Bridge the Gap** badge

---

### Module 6 · Addressing Ethics in Leadership & Exploring Destructive Leadership
**Key Concepts**: Ethical leadership · 5 Bases of Power · 6 Pillars of Character · Toxic triangle
**EI Games**: Level 4 due
**Platform opportunity**:
- **Power Base** selection at join (French & Raven, 1959): **Legitimate / Reward / Expert / Referent / Coercive** — displayed as badge chip on pin card
- Dashboard: "Ethical Leadership Snapshot" — % chose Expert or Referent (positive) vs Coercive (⚠️ flag as discussion prompt, not judgment)
- **Pillars of Character Poll** (Josephson Institute): professor pushes one of 6 scenario-based questions (Trustworthiness / Respect / Responsibility / Fairness / Caring / Citizenship) → students vote → results shown live with geographic breakdown on globe
- Quest: "Identify all 5 power bases among your connections" → **Power Mapper** badge (+75 XP)
- AI insight: *"What does it mean that students from [Region X] predominantly chose Referent power? How does cultural context shape power perception?"*

## Platform Architecture for Multi-Course Support

### Problem
Student World is currently hardcoded for OGL 360 (Intercultural Leadership). OGL 200 has different learning objectives, different form fields, different quest themes.

### Solution: Course Configuration Layer
A single `COURSE_CONFIG` object loaded based on URL param `?course=ogl200` or Canvas LTI custom param:

```
COURSE_CONFIG = {
  id: 'ogl200',
  title: 'Where We Lead From',       // vs "Where We Come From" for OGL 360
  subtitle: 'OGL 200 · Intro to Org Leadership · Dr. Hirshorn',
  joinFormFields: ['name', 'location', 'country', 'style', 'strength', 'insight'],
  leadershipValues: ['Authoritarian', 'Democratic', 'Laissez-faire'],  // or power bases
  strengthOptions: ['Communication', 'Empathy', 'Strategy', 'Execution', 'Relationship', 'Learning'],
  conflictStyles: ['Collaborating', 'Competing', 'Avoiding', 'Accommodating', 'Compromising'],
  aiPromptContext: 'OGL 200, Northouse leadership framework, Tuckman model, 5 Bases of Power',
  activeModuleQuests: [], // professor unlocks these per module
}
```

This allows Dr. Hirshorn to use the **same platform** for both courses, just switching the config.
